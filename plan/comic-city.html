Comic City: every child is a superhero

A maths app for children aged 3 to 10, told as an interactive comic in which the child is the hero. Every day is a new episode: a call for help comes in, a team of young heroes needs the child's maths, and the comic page itself is where the maths happens. Panels are groups, a long panel is a number line, panels in a row are time, and the page turn shows whether the answer works. Each finished day becomes an issue of the child's own comic, with their hero on the cover.

The careful learning rules are the same as in the first plan (a map of small skills with stars, five steps for every skill, the feedback rules, reviews, a parents' page). Everything the child sees and does is new: the world, the heroes, the day, the challenge, speed and the activities. The pictures are Gemini drafts; the storyboards are mock-ups.

2 · The big idea

The comic page is the maths

A comic page is already a maths picture: panels are groups, a row of panels is time passing, a gutter is a line that things cross. Comic City uses this. Each activity is a page whose panels are the maths the child is learning, and the page turn is how the world tests an answer.

Four rules that make it creative

1 · You are the hero

The child's hero is in every episode: on the map, in the finales, on the cover. Friends never give the answer: they react, and when the child is stuck they give a thinking tip.

2 · Make it, then turn the page

The child builds or predicts, and the page turn shows what really happens: the kart rolls, Zip lands on his roof, the suit turns the mixed colour.

3 · The scene answers a wrong try

A wrong answer has a funny result, never a mark: Mum cat looks for a kitten that is not there, the ball rolls away, the ice-cream van has gone home, the suit turns tomato red. Never red, never "wrong"; the try stays; after a second miss the working is shown. Stars count first tries only.

4 · Many right answers

Every way to make 12, the cube or the long box, any colour from the right fractions: the app keeps every way the child finds, and the heroes react to each.

3 · The heroes

Six young heroes, one rascal, and you

Each hero's power is a kind of maths. They are children too, from about 5 (Dot) to 10 (Mega), so every child can find one like them.

Dot

The smallest hero, about 5. Her fingertip glows: whatever she touches lights up with its number.

Helps with: counting with the youngest (Kitten Rescue, Flash Dots).

Zip

The fastest boy in the city, always running. Coaches speed on the roof of headquarters.

Helps with: adding and taking away (Rooftop Hops), speed (Speed Training, Bubble Pop).

Echo

Can make copies of herself, and every copy carries the same.

Helps with: multiplying and dividing (Echo's Copies).

Poly

Turns into any shape, flat or solid. Runs the Colour Lab.

Helps with: shapes and fractions (Missing Pieces, Colour Lab).

Tock

Wears the city's biggest wristwatch; her clock badge keeps the town on time.

Helps with: time (Clock Tower).

Mega

The biggest and gentlest hero, about 10. Grows ten times bigger, and back.

Helps with: big numbers and place value (Sticker Pack).

4 · A day

Today's episode

Every day the Hero Signal lights up: a short warm-up, then three calls for help on the city map, which the child answers in any order (the app chooses them: one skill to remember, one new power, one to practise), and finally Muddle's Mix-up. About 9 to 13 minutes, depending on age. Today's page colours in as the child goes: pencil sketches become inked, then coloured.

5 · The daily challenge

Muddle's Mix-up

The episode ends with one big page of the city, washed in Muddle's purple, with ten things he muddled: cakes cut unfairly, a shop sign with the wrong number, the bus-stop clock. The child fixes them in any order, and each fixed spot gets its colour back. Small, middle and big muddles are worth 1, 2 and 3 points (17 in all); only first tries score. The finished page is the last page of the day's issue.

6 · Speed

Zip's Speed Training

The daily warm-up is a run across the city's rooftops with facts the child already knows: every right answer is one leap. A see-through afterimage of the child's own best run runs alongside. Each family of facts has a speed rank (Rookie, Sidekick, Hero, Super Hero, Lightning), and the Power Grid shows each times fact turning gold when it is quick and sure on three different days. The fact stays still and the pad never moves; there is no clock under 8, and no ranking against other children.

8 · Rewards

Your own comic, your own costume

📚 The comic shelf

One issue a day, with the child's hero on the cover, the day's title and stars. Over a month the shelf fills; a month makes a volume with a special cover. Nothing on it can be bought.

🦸 The Costume Room

Coins for each part of the episode (about 45 a day, 55 at most) buy capes, masks, boots and gadgets. Skin, hair and face are always free. No mystery boxes, nothing expires, and nothing bought makes the maths easier.

📘 The Power Book

Every skill is a panel in the hero's Power Book: pencil when started, inked when the child can do it alone, coloured when it is still there a week later.

Parents get a separate page: skills learned, first tries, where help was needed, gold facts and time spent.
