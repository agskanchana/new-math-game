The game

Pom's Peeps ride a little train along the beach. The child presses the clay mat with one or more fingertips; a Peep pops out of every dent and hops into the train. The new wagon has ten windows in two rows of five, so every wagon is a ten-frame on wheels: an empty window shows what is missing, a full wagon is a ten.

Hands-on

Press with fingertips (several at once), drag a Peep, tap a wagon to send it.

The end of a round

The train toots and rolls away along the beach, full of Peeps.

How it plays

These frames are the real app today; the frames further down show the same game in other lessons and ages.

1Press the clay

The mat waits for fingertips; the empty train stands on the track.

🔊 “Press the clay with your fingers! How many Peeps can ride the train?”

2Peeps pop out

Every fingertip leaves a dent and a Peep pops out of it, counted aloud.

🔊 “Three Peeps!”

3Fill the empty seats

Some Peeps are already on board: how many more?

🔊 “Some Peeps are on the train already. Fill the empty seats!”

4One too many

A Peep with no seat waits by the engine until it is tapped home.

🔊 “Oh! That Peep has no seat. Tap it to send it home!”

The quick round (from age 6)

The Peeps are already on board: look at the wagons and type the answer (8 + 5, 3 × 4, 14 ÷ 3). Ten questions at 6-7, a 60-second round from 8; your time and your best on screen; only right answers count; a miss shows the answer and comes back later. No clock under 6.

The same game, other lessons and ages

The badge at the top left says the topic, the lesson and the age.

5Make 10 · age 5

Seven Peeps on board: the three empty windows glow. Three fingertips fill the wagon.

6Make 10 to add · age 6

8 + 5: two Peeps fill the first wagon, three go on: 10 + 3 = 13.

7Times is equal groups · age 8

The quick round: three wagons of four are already on board; type 12. The clock races your best.

8Divide with remainders · age 9

Fourteen Peeps, three wagons: four in each, and two wait on the platform.

From age 3 to 10

Age 3Pre-schoolCount 1 to 5: press, a Peep pops out, Pom counts aloud, each rides in its own window.
Age 4Pre-KCount to 10; one more and one fewer; put together and take away up to 5.
Age 5KindergartenMake 10: how many more fill the wagon? Teen numbers: a full wagon and some more. Stories at the stations.
Age 6Grade 1Add through 10 (fill the wagon first), tens and ones to 120, stories. Quick rounds begin.
Age 7Grade 2Odd and even: do the Peeps all sit in pairs? Equal groups: the same in every wagon.
Age 8Grade 3Times as equal groups (3 wagons of 4), sharing fairly, making groups.
Age 9Grade 4Sharing with remainders: two Peeps wait for the next train.
Age 10Grade 5A fraction of a number (a third of the Peeps get off); a fraction is a division.

What changes from lesson to lesson

Numbers

up to 5 at 3, to 10 at 4, to 20 at 5, then tens, and groups of up to 10

What you see

only Peeps at first; then numbers under the wagons; then the number sentence

Help

Pom counts aloud; empty windows glow; after a miss the windows are counted one by one

How you answer

fingertips and dragging; the number pad from 5

The story

who gets on and off at which station
