The idea

From Grade 3 the lessons are taught on practice screens, not in games: one calm Clay Bay screen for each kind of question, the scene soft behind, the maths on a clay slate in the middle, a friend at the side with a tip, and the number pad. Every skill climbs the same steps: see it with a model (groups of Peeps, an area model, a number line, a bar), fill one part, do it all, check it (guess first, spot the mistake), word problems, then the quick round for speed. Stars, coins, the Hatch and the Play Park all stay, so learning still earns the fun.

How you answer

Tap one of three answers; type on the number pad (with / and . keys); drag a point along a line; shade parts.

The end of a round

Stars for the round; three stars twice, on two different days, and the lesson is learned.

How it plays

One screen for each step, from four lessons, drawn from the app's own clay pictures.

Times is equal groups, age 8: three groups of four Peeps; one tap answers 12.

3Do it all

Fractions on a number line, age 8: where is the flag? Type 3/4.

4Word problem

Take away decimals, age 10: Digsy's rope, with a bar to help.

5Quick

Times 6 and 7, age 8: ten in a row against your best, with a streak.

The quick round (from age 6)

The same screens at speed: ten in a row or 60 seconds, your time against your best, a streak; the facts in Volcano Pop.

What changes from lesson to lesson

The model

Peeps in groups, an area model, a number line, a bar, a place value chart, a clock, coins: the same clay pictures as the games

The step

see it, fill one part, do it all, check it, word problems, quick

Help

the friend's tip after a miss; the working after two; never "wrong"

How you answer

one tap, the number pad (with / and .), a drag along a line
