The game

The friends have things to do at set times: the ice-cream boat comes at half past three. The child turns the big clay clock's hands, and the hour hand moves with the minute hand like a real clock. When the time is right the tower bongs and the bay acts it out; a wrong time shows itself (half past seven: the moon is out).

Hands-on

Turn the minute hand (or the hour hand); read the clock and type.

The end of a round

Your day in Clay Bay: the day's times on a time line.

How it plays

A storyboard of the game in one lesson, drawn from the app's own clay pictures.

1Half past three

The big clock says three o'clock; the tower's small clock follows it.

🔊 “The ice-cream boat comes at half past three! Set the big clock.”

2Turn the hand

The minute hand goes round to the 6; the little hand moves halfway to 4.

3Bong!

The right time: the boat comes in.

4A wrong time shows itself

Half past seven: the sun has gone and the moon is out.

🔊 “That is half past seven: the sun has gone! Sunset is at seven o'clock.”

5The quick round

Read the clock, type 25. Timed.

6Your day

The day's times on a time line.

The quick round (from age 6)

Read the clock and type the time (7 : 25), or how long something took. Ten questions at 6-7, a 60-second round from 8; your time and your best on screen; only right answers count; a miss shows the answer and comes back later. No clock under 6.

What changes from lesson to lesson

The hands

the hour hand only, then both; 5-minute steps, then every minute

The job

set a time, read a time, how long, a timetable

Help

the bay acts the time out; after a miss the hands are moved slowly to the right time

How you answer

turning the hands; the number pad for the quick round
